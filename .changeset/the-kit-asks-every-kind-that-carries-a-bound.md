The conformance kit asks every kind that carries a native constraint, instead of the three someone
listed.

"Declared rules reach the control" named `text`, `textarea` and `number`, and between them four
attributes. The kind it did not name is where the defect lived: a Vue slider whose declared bounds
never reached its `<input type="range">` answered Home with 0 and End with 100 on a field declared
10–20 — and the suite reported it CONFORMANT, because nothing asked.

What a kind is owed now comes from `nativeConstraintAttributes`, the same door the projection uses,
with the value the fixture holds passed in because it is part of the answer: a slider's track spans
what the field holds where nothing declared a bound, and its `step` gives way to a value that is not
on one. A kind covered by that door is asked the day the contract says it carries something, rather
than the day someone remembers to edit the kit.

**`step` is not asked, and the section says so rather than appearing to have asked.** A field
declares rules through validators and the validator vocabulary has no `step` — the engine refuses to
build one — so a slider's step arrives by a road no `rules`-driven check can reach. That is a gap in
what the contract can be asked, not in this section.

Verified against the defect it exists for: restoring the Vue slider's missing route turns the section
red by name, on both bounds, and every other adapter stays green.
