The conformance kit says what a section could not reach, instead of ticking it.

"State matrix" printed `✓ 6 of 150 pairs asserted, 144 undrivable`. A tick is what a reader acts on,
so the count read as detail rather than as the finding it was: a section that judged four per cent of
its subject and reported the same mark as one that judged all of it. The five components repaired in
`@modyra/vue` today were exactly the kind of defect that section exists to catch, and it was blind by
abstention rather than by absence.

A partly reached section now carries its own mark and its own lines, counted per kind — `datepicker:
9 state(s) this renderer cannot be driven into` names the next piece of work, where `144 undrivable`
named none. The verdict carries it too, so a reader who takes only the last line is not told
something the numbers above it contradict.

What it surfaced on the first run is the argument for it: **plain and lit drive 150 of 150 pairs;
angular, react and Vue drive almost none.** Two adapters have been exercising every declared state
for as long as the section existed, and three have been collecting the same tick for a run that
looked at six pairs.
