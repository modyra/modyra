A popup that opens says so

`overlayLifecycleTransition` — the one policy every adapter opens and closes by — returns an
announcement alongside the new state, and the words are published in five languages. Plain, Lit and
Angular say them. This renderer computed the answer and dropped it: six kinds opened a panel and
nothing on the page said anything.

Belt and braces rather than a blackout, which is why it went unnoticed: `aria-expanded` on the opener
already changes, so a reader that *inspects* the control learns the state. What was missing is being
told — the difference between information available on request and information delivered when it
changes, which for a panel appearing elsewhere on the page is the difference between noticing and not.

Said from the one place this package opens and closes panels, so all six kinds answer together and a
seventh gets it by existing. Said only when the state moves: announced on mount too, every field on
a page would report itself closed before anybody had done anything.

The words are read at the moment of the change rather than captured, so a document that switches
language mid-page gets the language it is speaking then. **Two kinds have no language to read**:
`select` and `multiselect` declare no `locale`, alone among the kinds that open a panel, so they take
the default table. Recorded here rather than papered over — a document that switches language gets
English in those two and nowhere else.
