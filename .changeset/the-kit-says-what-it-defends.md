Every conformance section names the public claim it defends.

The adversarial suite binds its checks to claims as data and the browser tier is being promoted to
it; this kit bound nothing. Its sections had names of their own and none said which promise it was
keeping — so a run could find six real defects, as one did, and not one of them had a name anybody
could look up. The registry is meant to be the vocabulary of what this project knows how to find, and
a tool outside it is a tool whose findings cannot be counted.

The link is data and it is **checked**: a section whose title has no entry stops the run rather than
quietly losing its claim, which is what a rename would otherwise do.

**Three sections say `[no claim names this]`, and that is the finding rather than a gap to paper
over.** Renderer equivalence defends the thesis of this phase — that renderers agree with the
catalogue rather than with each other — and the registry has no entry for it. The second channel is
newer than the registry (ADR 0205). And identity, whether two instances can share an id, is the
subject of a whole family of open findings with no word for it. Registering a claim belongs to
whoever keeps the charter; inventing one here would have been this tool naming its own promises.
